Reference motion-studio

Motion

Every animation answers one question: what does it communicate? Hierarchy, story, feedback or a state change. If you cannot say it in a sentence, cut it.

Easing and springs

  • Closed-form spring for entrances: damping ratio ζ 0.8 to 0.9, ω about 9 (0.5 to 0.7 s). At most a tiny overshoot. No bouncy easing.
  • GSAP names that match: expo.out for entrances (0.5 s), power2.in for exits (0.26 s), power3.inOut for rules and wipes.
  • Enter moving, leave accelerating. Exits are faster than entrances.
  • Stagger 40 to 80 ms between items, or put each item on its spoken word.
function spring(z, w) { // normalised so it ends exactly at 1
  const wd = w * Math.sqrt(1 - z * z);
  const raw = (t) => 1 - Math.exp(-z * w * t) * (Math.cos(wd * t) + ((z * w) / wd) * Math.sin(wd * t));
  const end = raw(1);
  return (t) => (t <= 0 ? 0 : t >= 1 ? 1 : raw(t) / end);
}
const tw = (t, a, b, ease) => ease(Math.min(1, Math.max(0, (t - a) / (b - a))));

Kinetic type

  • Word enter: rise 0.42 em out of an 8 px blur, opacity 0 → 1, 0.5 s expo.out, starting 60 ms before the spoken word.
  • Caption exit (content swap): opacity 0, y −14 px, blur 6 px, 0.26 s power2.in; the next caption starts right after.
  • A reference line under a number fades up 0.25 s after the last word.
  • Hook lines stack (each phrase on its own line); the last word of the hook can carry the accent.
  • Never put will-change on text the camera scales (blurry text).

Camera (for screen content)

  • Keys with holds: a list of {t, scale, focusX, focusY, ease}. Between two moves there is always a hold. The camera only moves when a spoken word motivates it; never idle drift.
  • Screen-recorder zoom: zoom the content inside a fixed window frame (frame stays, content scales), focus point in recorded CSS pixels, clamp so no edge shows.
  • Interpolate zoom in log space (exp(lerp(log a, log b, u))) so the speed feels constant.
  • One named move per shot: push in to the composer, pull back to the conversation. Never zoom in and straight back out.
  • Typical scales: 1.0 whole screen, 1.4 to 1.6 a panel or table, 1.8 to 2.1 a button or field.
  • Keys must stay in time order; when hold times derive from word timings, clamp with Math.max(previous).
  • Update the camera at the start of each frame: overlays (spotlight, cursor, labels) are projected from it.

Spotlight

  • Dim everything but the region (box-shadow 0 0 0 3000px rgba(dark, 0.42)), 3 px accent outline, radius 12, padding 8 px.
  • Rects in recorded CSS px, mapped through the camera every frame.
  • Liquid move: when the spotlight jumps from one row to the next, the edge in the direction of travel leads (0.30 s) and the trailing edge follows (0.42 s, starting 0.1 s later), so the highlight stretches and settles.
  • Fade in 0.3 s; when a spot hands over to the next one directly, glide instead of blinking.

Cursor

  • SVG arrow with a soft drop shadow, constant screen size.
  • Appears 1.15 s before the click, glides in 0.9 s with inOut, tilts up to 12° toward its travel direction and straightens on arrival.
  • Press: scale −15 % for about 80 ms at the click; an accent ring expands from 0.3× to 1.3× and fades over 0.5 s.
  • Leaves 0.3 to 0.55 s after the click. Keep it inside the frame at every zoom and off the key text of the final state.
  • Every UI change after a click happens 80 to 150 ms after the press.

Pacing

  • Hold important frames: a number or the CTA needs 1.5 to 2 s of stillness.
  • Alternate long and short beats so it breathes; cut on the beat grid of the music.
  • Reserve showpieces (a big reveal, a flood) for two or three moments.

Motion blur

Render 4 subframes per output frame centred on the frame time and average them (8 for fast moves with a shutter of about 0.7 of a frame, see gotchas.md): ffmpeg ... -vf "tmix=frames=4,select='not(mod(n+1\,4))',setpts=N/(60*TB)" with the page seeked at t + (j − 1.5)/(fps·4).

Gotchas

  • Never set opacity or filter on a preserve-3d element; fade a wrapper instead.
  • Text inside a morphing container needs its own enter and exit timing or old and new text overlap.
  • A seek-based renderer must not depend on state carried between frames. Compute everything from t.

Measure motion, do not guess

When a move "pauses then snaps", stop retrying eased variants. Sample the timing function (a pure function of frame) and plot value and velocity over the move: inspect.mjs plot (see skills/motion-review), or in a scratch script. Look for a zero-velocity run in the middle of a move, kinks, and false overshoot; then fix the cause and plot again. Why: in one production write-up five plausible fixes failed over about an hour; a velocity plot showed zero velocity for 16 percent of the move in 30 seconds. The cause was a bob wave covering only the rise phase, not the element's whole lifetime. Evidence: one detailed first-hand article with numbers (F-A3, F-B5).

Buoyant bob. A rising element keeps drifting through its peak instead of freezing: a cubic ease plus a small sine bob whose span is the whole lifetime of the element, not just the rise. Helper: see studio/src/lib/motion.ts (buoyant()). Use for floating cards and badges, not for UI that should feel mechanical.

Camera speed as a table

For any glide, write the camera language as speed shares of cruise at timestamps and integrate to position, instead of position keys:

8.30.12 (easing out)

(Example from a 3D fly-over prompt; your numbers differ.) Interpolate with smoothstep and integrate analytically (see learnings: speed-curve camera). Zoom runs in log space; rise and dive are separate eased passes. Each scene starts at the zoom the last one ended on. Check: inspect.mjs flow on a 30 fps draft. Speed must never dip and zoom must not turn into zoom-in during a flight; a surge or a stop that stills cannot show shows up as a dip in the flow plot. Why: the table makes the brief's camera language reviewable and flow measurement catches "no surges, no stops". Evidence: precise in the prompt, film posted; flow-test results not shown (F-B4). Integrated speed curves are already in learnings; the new part is the table and the test.

Rhythm and type-sync recipes

Why: cheap, deterministic, look authored rather than templated. Evidence: documented with demos; parameters tuned on placeholder material (F-A16).

  • Karaoke fill (voice-led films, type hooks): each word fills left to right with a soft edge at speaking rate; current word in accent, finished word in ink; a pointer underline on the active word; word times from the voice; 4 to 10 frames of gap for breath. Studio fit: yes.
  • Weight pump (type hooks): a variable-font weight envelope on the beat (about 250 to 900); glyphs set in slots laid out at the heaviest weight so the line never reflows; beats must be spaced wider than the decay window. Studio fit: yes. Remember learnings: set font-weight on the glyphs too, not just the measurement.
  • Frame-rate shift (optional spice): a mover steps every 3 frames (about 69 px per step, a different pose each step), then switches in one frame to every-frame motion with hard overshoot; the change of cadence is the highlight. Under 30 px per step it reads as dropped frames, and any tween on the switch kills it.
  • Trailer bumper (optional spice): three framings of 9 frames each, zero transitions, each pushing in 1 to 4 percent so it is not a slideshow, then 6 frames of black, then the title.

Transition recipes (velocity-matched cut, occluder, impact slam, light-leak, line carry, "X becomes Y") are in transitions.md. Failure-derived checks (8 subframes for fast moves, minimum fade length) are in gotchas.md.
