Transitions: "X becomes Y", velocity handoff, hidden cuts

Complements the table in motion.md. Components: see studio/src/lib/transitions.tsx (Remotion); the HTML engine writes the recipe by hand. Numbers below come from the authors' frame measurements of other films and were tuned on placeholder material: they are starting points, not laws.

1. Every scene is made out of the previous one

Rule: the storyboard's transition column never says "cut" or "fade". It says "X becomes Y": the outgoing object, what it carries (label, words, colour, position), the incoming object. Why: naming the shared element is what removes the crossfade default, and it is checkable on a contact sheet (does frame N-1 contain the thing frame N grows from?). Evidence: shown in two finished films (F-B3); our showreel inspiration already adopted it. Scope: strict for UI and product pieces. For type pieces a hard cut onto a page that is already set is still fine (learnings): then the column says "hard cut, page already set" and the cut sits on a beat.

Morph vocabulary (write these in the column):

NameWhat happens
IrisA circle clip opens or closes on the element the viewer was looking at
Flood and contractA solid shape floods past all corners (0.3 to 0.35 s), then contracts into the next scene's first element
Accordion squeezePanels fold to a line or strip, and the line is the next scene's rule or bar
Line carryA rule leaves the frame and the camera follows it; it turns corners and draws the next card (section 3D)
Bezel or mat growsA device bezel grows out of the screen edge, or a mat grows from the photo edge, into the next surface
Text rises from a mask lineThe next caption starts as the line the previous one sat on
Label carryA button carries its label into the next page's heading

2. Velocity-matched cut

The outgoing shot accelerates one way and is cut at its fastest frame; the incoming shot starts already moving the same way and brakes to rest, so momentum crosses the edit. Why: it turns "smooth transition" into numbers and explains why some dissolve-free cuts feel dead. Evidence: doc-level, numbers are the author's measurement at 60 fps (F-A6).

ParameterValue (at 60 fps; scale by 60/fps for px per frame)
Exitspeeds up about 1.1x to 1.2x per frame over the last 16 to 24 frames, ends at 25 to 40 px/frame, never above 60
Entrystarts at 30 to 55 px/frame, decays about 0.92 to 0.94 per frame
Text on a moving shotunder 20 px/frame by the time it is read
Motion blurthe cut is an act boundary: subframes never straddle it

Companion rule: a hard cut works when subject, scale, direction and material continue across it. Helper: see studio/src/lib/transitions.tsx (velocity-matched cut) and studio/src/lib/motion.ts; check the result with inspect.mjs flow (speed must not dip at the cut) and inspect.mjs plot for a single value.

3. Hidden-cut and no-cut recipes

Why: each carries a cause before the cut and a result after, and the failure modes are written down. Evidence: doc-level cards (F-A7); re-skin to our tokens (no source brand colours, one accent).

A. Occluder cut. A soft, out-of-focus foreground object sweeps across; the hard cut happens inside the frames where it covers the screen. About 28 px of drag before, a settle after, so it reads as one take.

B. Impact slam (comparison moments only). Two half-screens with a slanted seam collide: white flash 3 frames, screen shake about 16 px decaying over about 5 frames, a stamp, all on the same frame (a one-frame offset scatters it). Counts against the film's cap of 3 full-frame beat hits (audio.md).

C. Light-leak burn. A diagonal wash of light ramps up slowly (ease-in), the cut hides at the peak, it recedes faster. The old picture must also wash out (brightness up, contrast down) or the leak reads as a sticker. Studio fit: tint the wash with the accent or a warm tint of the brand neutral, no flare streaks, no glow clichés; if it is not clearly better than a plain flood, use the flood.

D. Line carry (no cut at all). A progress bar extends out of frame, the camera follows the line, the line turns corners and draws the next scene's card frame. Pin the pen tip near x = 1480 (at 1920 wide) so it never leaves the frame or lags.

Banned, unchanged

Cross-dissolves between unrelated shots, wipes and star transitions, zooming in and straight back out. A hard cut still needs a page that is already set.
